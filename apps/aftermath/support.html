Support guide for using Aftermath: Money Left to Spend.

Aftermath is a simple, private way to see how much money you really have left to spend between one payday and the next, once everything you still owe has been taken into account. Everything below explains what each screen does and how to use it – no technical knowledge needed. If you've never used the app before, start with Getting started below.

👋Getting started

The first time you open Aftermath, a short set-up guide walks you through everything the app needs. Each step shows a tip first – tap OK to dismiss it and carry on. You won't see the guide again unless you reset the app from Settings (more on that below).

1. Welcome

A quick introduction to the app. Tap Next to begin set-up. If you've used Aftermath before and have a backup file, tap Restore from backup instead – this loads your backup and takes you straight to the Home screen, skipping the rest of the guide.

2. How do you get paid?

Everything in Aftermath is organised around your pay period – the time from one payday to the next – rather than a calendar month. Choose how often you're paid:

  • Weekly – pick the day of the week you're paid.
  • Fortnightly – pick the day of the week you're paid, plus the date of any recent payment, so the app knows which weeks your paydays fall in.
  • Semi-monthly – pick the two days of the month you're paid (for example, the 1st and the 16th).
  • Monthly – choose a specific day of the month, the last working day of the month (Monday to Friday), or the last occurrence of a particular day (for example, the last Friday).

Your payday is always the first day of a new pay period. If you choose a day that doesn't exist in some months (such as the 31st), the app uses the last day of that month instead.

3. Starting balance & currency

Enter roughly how much you're paid each period. Don't worry if it varies – it's just used to fill in the starting balance for each new pay period, and you can adjust it every time. Then set your currency symbol (for example £, $ or €).

4. Recurring expenses

Next, the guide takes you to the real Recurring Expenses screen so you can add a few of your regular bills. You don't have to enter them all now – add some so you can see how things work, then tap Next. See the Recurring Expenses screen section for how to add them.

5. Home

Finally, you'll be shown around the Home screen with a few more tips. Once you've dismissed the last one, you're all set. While the guide is running, the menu is dimmed so you can't wander off part-way through.

🏠Home screen

This is the main screen and the one you'll use most. Its title shows the dates of your current pay period (for example, "12 Jul – 25 Jul 2026"). You can always get back here by opening the menu (the three lines in the top-left corner) and choosing the pay period at the top.

Current balance

The large figure at the top. Tap it to type in a new amount – it should match what your bank account shows right now. Update it whenever you check your bank balance.

Estimated remainder

The figure underneath is the heart of the app: your current balance, minus every expense still left to pay this period. In other words, how much you really have left to spend (or save). It updates automatically as you make changes, and turns red if you're heading into the negative.

Recurring expenses

Your regular bills that fall due during this pay period, filled in automatically from the Recurring Expenses screen and listed in the order they're due. Each has an amount you can tap to change for this period only – for example, if this month's electricity bill came in higher than usual.

Ad hoc expenses

One-off or irregular costs for this period – think birthdays, car repairs, a big purchase, or your food shop. Tap + Add ad hoc expense, give it a name and an amount. Ad hoc expenses belong to the current period only: the list starts empty again each new pay period. Tap the red bin icon to delete one (you'll be asked to confirm).

Marking an expense as paid

Once you've paid something, tap the ✓ button next to it. This sets its amount to zero, so it no longer counts against your remainder. You can also just type in a smaller amount if you've paid part of it. Changing an expense only changes your remainder – it never changes your current balance, since that should always come from your bank.

Tip: a typical routine is to pay a bill, tick it off, then update your current balance to match your bank. Your remainder then shows exactly what's still free to spend.

Hide completed expenses

Turn this switch on to hide anything already marked as paid, so the lists only show what's still outstanding. The app remembers your choice.

🔁Recurring Expenses screen

This is where you keep your list of regular bills – rent, subscriptions, insurance and so on. Tap the menu and choose Recurring Expenses. The app uses this list to fill in the Home screen automatically at the start of each pay period.

Adding an expense

Tap + Add expense at the bottom of the list, then fill in:

  • Name and Amount.
  • Frequency – Weekly, Monthly or Annual. This is separate from how often you're paid, so you can be paid weekly and still have a monthly rent payment.
  • When it's due – the day of the week (weekly), the day of the month (monthly), or the month and day (annual).
  • Start date and, optionally, an end date (tick Has end date) for something that stops, such as a loan's final payment.

Tap Save. If the new expense falls due in your current pay period, it appears on the Home screen straight away.

How expenses land in your pay periods

  • A weekly expense is counted once for every time its day falls in the pay period – so a £10 weekly expense in a pay period with four Fridays shows as £40.
  • A monthly expense appears in whichever pay period contains its due day. If you're paid weekly or fortnightly, that means it shows in just one of the periods that make up the month.
  • An annual expense appears only in the pay period that contains its due date.

Editing or deleting an expense

Tap an expense to edit it, or tap the red bin icon to delete it (you'll be asked to confirm). Changes and deletions here take effect from your next pay period – the current period on the Home screen is left as it is, so you can adjust that directly if you need to.

Filtering and reordering

Use the All / Weekly / Monthly / Annual buttons at the top to show just one type. While viewing All, you can drag an expense up or down using the ≡ handle on the right to rearrange the list.

📅Starting a new pay period

When your current pay period ends, the next time you open the Home screen Aftermath will ask whether you'd like to start the new one. It never does this without asking.

  • Tap Start new period, then check or adjust the starting balance (it's filled in from your default in Settings) and tap Confirm.
  • The period that just ended is saved to History exactly as it stood.
  • The new period's recurring expenses are filled in from your Recurring Expenses list, and the ad hoc list starts empty.

If you're not ready (for example, your pay hasn't arrived yet), tap Not yet. You'll be asked again the next time you return to the Home screen.

📖History screen

A record of every pay period you've finished, most recent first. Tap the menu and choose History. It stays empty until your first pay period has ended and you've started the next one.

Each period shows its dates and closing balance. Tap a period to expand it and see its final remainder plus the full list of recurring and ad hoc expenses as they stood when it ended. History is read-only, so nothing here can be changed by accident.

🔮Forecast screen

A look ahead at future pay periods, to help you spot an expensive month coming. Tap the menu and choose Forecast. It has a purple tint to remind you it's a projection, not real figures.

  • Use ‹ Prev and Next › to move between future pay periods.
  • Each forecast starts from your default starting balance (from Settings) and subtracts the recurring expenses due in that period, giving a projected remainder.
  • Weekly expenses that occur more than once show how many times, e.g. "(×4)".
  • Ad hoc expenses aren't included, since there's no way to know them in advance.

Nothing on this screen is saved, and it never affects your Home screen or History.

⚙️Settings screen

Tap the menu and choose Settings to personalise the app. Each section has its own ⓘ icon you can tap for a quick reminder of what it does.

Appearance

Choose Light or Dark, or System to follow your phone's own setting.

Balance & currency

Change the default starting balance used when a new pay period begins, and the currency symbol shown next to every amount.

Pay frequency

Change how often you're paid, using the same options as during set-up, then tap Save pay frequency. This ends your current pay period straight away – it's saved to History as it stands – and starts a new one under the new schedule, asking you for a starting balance just like a normal new period.

Backup & restore

Aftermath keeps your data only on your phone, so a backup is the only way to get it back if your phone is lost, replaced or reset. The line at the top shows when you last backed up.

Export backup saves a copy of your data to a file, which you can then save anywhere you like – your phone, a cloud storage app, email, wherever you choose. You'll be asked what to include:

  • Full backup – everything, including your current pay period and all of your History.
  • Configuration only – just your settings and Recurring Expenses list. Handy for setting the app up the same way on another phone without bringing your figures along.

Restore backup lets you pick a previously exported file and load it back in. This replaces whatever the backup contains (for a configuration-only backup, just your settings and Recurring Expenses), so you'll be asked to confirm first.

Tip: backup files contain your financial figures and aren't encrypted, so keep them somewhere you trust.

Backup reminders

Turn this on to be reminded to back up, and use the − and + buttons with week, month or year to choose how often (for example, every 2 weeks). When a backup is due, the app shows a reminder the next time you open it, with the option to back up there and then or be reminded later. These reminders appear inside the app only – they aren't phone notifications.

Reset app

Completely wipes the app – your Recurring Expenses, current pay period and all History – and returns every setting to its default, then runs the set-up guide again. This cannot be undone. If you haven't backed up recently, the app will offer to make a backup first.
