Privacy Policy for RSS News Reader

Last updated: 30 October 2026

In short: RSS News Reader does not collect, transmit, or sell your data to us. There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, and no cloud sync. Your subscriptions, categories, reading history, and settings stay in a local database on your device. To do its job the App does connect to the internet – to download the news feeds you subscribe to, and to search Feedly when you look for new feeds – and those connections are described below.

Who this policy covers

This policy applies to the RSS News Reader mobile app for iOS and Android (the "App"), published by Simon Goodson ("we", "us"). It explains what information the App handles and what happens to it.

Information the App handles

The App does not require you to create an account, and does not ask for your name, email address, or any other identifying information. The only data it works with is:

  • Your subscriptions – the address of each news feed you subscribe to, along with the feed's name, description, website, and icon as published by the feed, and whether you've switched it off.
  • Your feed categories – the category names you create, and which category each subscription belongs to.
  • Articles and reading history – for each article in your feeds, its headline, author, publication date, and links to the article and its picture, plus whether and when you read it (so the App can list your read articles, most recent first). The App does not store the full text of articles.
  • App preferences – settings such as light or dark mode, how long to keep read articles, and your backup reminder schedule, so the App remembers how you like it configured.

The App does not access your location, contacts, photos, camera, or microphone. There are no analytics libraries, crash-reporting services, advertising SDKs, or third-party trackers built into the App.

Where your data is stored

All of the data described above is stored in a local database on your device only. We do not operate a server, and the App has no connection to any backend of ours – there isn't one. We never receive your subscriptions, your searches, or what you read.

Internet connections the App makes

A news reader has to fetch the news, so unlike a fully offline app, RSS News Reader connects directly from your device to other services. Each of these is a normal web request, similar to visiting a website in your browser. As with any web request, the service receiving it can see your device's IP address (which may indicate your approximate location) and basic technical details such as the App's name and version.

  • The websites you subscribe to. When the App refreshes – each time you open it, and whenever you pull down on the Home screen – it downloads each of your feeds directly from the website that publishes it. Pictures and icons shown next to articles are also downloaded from the publisher (or the image service it uses) and kept in a temporary cache on your device.
  • Websites you look up. When you type a web address into the Add New Subscription screen, the App visits that address to find its feed, and may check a few standard feed locations on the same website (such as /feed or /rss).
  • Feedly. When you type a name or topic (rather than a web address) into the Add New Subscription screen, the text you typed is sent to Feedly's public feed search service to find matching feeds. Feedly does not receive anything else from the App – not your subscriptions, your reading history, or any identifier. Each time you open that screen, the App also makes a quick check that Feedly is reachable; this check contains no search text. Feedly's handling of these requests is covered by Feedly's privacy policy.
  • Articles you open. Tapping an article opens it in your phone's built-in browser view (Safari on iPhone, Chrome or your default browser on Android), inside the App. From that point you are visiting the publisher's website, and it may use cookies or other tracking under its own privacy policy, exactly as it would in your normal browser.

The App always uses an encrypted (https) connection when a website offers one. A few websites only publish their feeds over an unencrypted (http) connection; for those, the App uses http, which means the feed and any pictures from it travel unencrypted and could be seen by the networks they pass through. Search requests to Feedly are always encrypted.

Each website and Feedly are independent of us, and their own privacy policies apply to the requests they receive. If you don't want a particular website to receive these requests, switch off or remove that subscription.

Backups

The App lets you export a backup of your data on demand. When you do, the backup file is created in the App's private storage on your device, then your device's standard share sheet opens and you choose where that file goes – saved to your device, a cloud storage app you already use, emailed to yourself, or anywhere else your device offers. The temporary copy is deleted as soon as the share sheet closes. We never see this file and have no way to access it. Restoring a backup works the same way in reverse: you choose the file, and it's read directly from your device.

Backup files are plain, unencrypted text and contain your subscriptions, categories, the articles you've read, and your settings, so please keep them somewhere you trust.

Importing subscriptions

You can import a list of subscriptions exported from another feed reader (an OPML file). You choose the file using your device's standard file picker; it is read directly on your device and is not sent anywhere. The feeds listed in it are then added to your subscriptions and fetched in the same way as any other subscription, as described above.

Reminders

Optional backup reminders are shown as a prompt inside the App the next time you open it once a backup is due. The App does not send notifications, and no push-notification service or third party is involved.

Data retention and deletion

Your data stays on your device for as long as you keep the App installed, or until you remove it yourself. In particular:

  • Articles you have read are deleted automatically once they are older than the period you choose in Settings (30 days unless you change it). Unread articles are kept.
  • Removing a subscription deletes it along with all of its saved articles.
  • Deleting a category removes it from any subscriptions in it; the subscriptions themselves are kept.
  • Resetting the App from Settings wipes all data and returns settings to their defaults.

Uninstalling the App also removes all of its local data from your device. Any backup files you have exported and saved elsewhere are not affected – those are yours to keep or delete.

Children's privacy

The App does not knowingly collect any information from anyone, regardless of age, because it does not collect information at all in the sense of transmitting it to us or storing it outside your device. It is not directed at children. The content of the feeds you subscribe to comes from their publishers, not from us.
