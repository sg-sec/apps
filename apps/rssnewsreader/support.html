Support & Help

Support guide for using RSS News Reader.

RSS News Reader gathers the latest articles from the websites, blogs and news sources you follow into one simple list, newest first, with everything you've already read cleared away. There's no account to create and your data stays on your phone. Everything below explains what each screen does and how to use it – no technical knowledge needed. If you've never used the app before, start with Getting started below.

👋Getting started

The first time you open RSS News Reader, a welcome screen explains what the app does. Tap Next to carry on. You won't see it again unless you reset the app from Settings.

You'll then land on the Home screen, which is empty until you subscribe to something. Tap Add a subscription to add your first feed – see the Add New Subscription screen section for how.

Moving from Newsify or another feed reader? You can import all of your subscriptions at once from Settings – see Import Subscriptions.

To move between screens, open the menu by tapping the three lines in the top-left corner, or by swiping in from the left edge of the screen.

Tip: if you'd like to group your feeds – for example News, Tech and Sport – create your categories first in Settings, so you can choose one as you add each feed.

🏠Home screen

This is the main screen and the one you'll use most. It lists every article you haven't read yet from all of your subscriptions, newest first. Each article shows its picture (or the feed's icon if it doesn't have one), headline, the name of the feed and author, and how long ago it was published.

Reading an article

Tap an article to open it. It opens in a browser view inside the app – close it (with Done or the ✕ in the corner) to come straight back to your list. Once you've opened an article it counts as read and disappears from the list. You can find it again on the Read Articles screen.

Clearing an article without reading it

Not interested in an article? Swipe it to the left until ✓ Clear appears, then let go. It's marked as read and disappears from the list without being opened. If you let go before it's swiped far enough, it slides back into place and nothing changes.

Tip: if you use VoiceOver or TalkBack, the same option is available as the Clear without reading action on each article.

Getting new articles

The app checks all of your feeds for new articles every time you open it. To check again at any time, pull the list down from the top and let go. If any feeds couldn't be checked, a short note under the filter says how many – see Troubleshooting.

Filtering by category

Use the drop-down at the top to show only the articles from one category, or Uncategorised for feeds that aren't in a category. Choose All to see everything again. The drop-down only offers categories once you've created some in Settings. Your choice is remembered, so Home opens on the same category next time.

Mark all read

Tap Mark all read to clear the list in one go (you'll be asked to confirm). If you're filtering by a category, only that category's articles are marked as read.

When there's nothing left to read, the screen tells you you're all caught up.

📚Read Articles screen

Every article you've opened or cleared, most recently read first, so you can go back to something you read earlier. Tap the menu and choose Read Articles. Each article shows when you read it, and the drop-down at the top filters by category, just like the Home screen.

  • Tap an article to open it again. It stays in this list.
  • Swipe an article to the left until Mark unread appears, then let go, to send it back to the Home screen as unread. (With VoiceOver or TalkBack, use the Mark as unread action.)

Read articles are kept for the period set under Articles in Settings (30 days to begin with), then deleted automatically.

➕Add New Subscription screen

This is where you find and add new feeds. Tap the menu and choose Add New Subscription. There's a single search box, and you can type either of two things into it:

  • A website or feed address – for example bbc.co.uk/news or arstechnica.com. You don't need to know the exact address of the feed: give the app any page from the website and it will find the feed the website offers.
  • A name or topic – for example BBC News or gardening. The app searches Feedly, a large public directory of news feeds, and lists matching feeds with their number of followers and how often they publish.

Tap Search (or the search key on your keyboard) to see the results.

Subscribing

Tap a result to select it. It expands to show a category drop-down – choose a category, or leave it as Uncategorised – then tap Subscribe. The feed's latest articles appear on the Home screen straight away. Feeds you're already subscribed to are marked ✓ Subscribed.

If Feedly search is unavailable

Searching by name or topic needs an internet connection to Feedly. If the app can't reach Feedly, a note at the top of the screen explains why; tap Retry to check again. You can still add a feed by typing its website address while Feedly is unavailable.

🗂️Manage Subscriptions screen

A list of every feed you've subscribed to, in alphabetical order. Tap the menu and choose Manage Subscriptions. Each feed shows its category and how many unread articles it has. Use the drop-down at the top to show just one category.

Switching a feed off

Use the switch on the right to turn a feed off without removing it. While it's off, the app stops checking it and its articles are hidden from the Home screen. Turn it back on and its articles reappear, read or unread just as you left them.

Changing a feed's category, or removing it

Tap a feed to expand it. You'll see its web address, when it was last updated, and a Category drop-down to move it to a different category. Tap Remove subscription to delete the feed and all of its saved articles (you'll be asked to confirm).

A feed marked ⚠ Couldn't update had a problem the last time the app checked it. Expand it to see what went wrong.

⚙️Settings screen

Tap the menu and choose Settings to personalise the app. Each section has its own ⓘ icon you can tap for a quick reminder of what it does.

Feed Categories

Create categories to organise your feeds: type a name into the box and tap Add. Tap the ✕ next to a category to delete it (you'll be asked to confirm). Deleting a category doesn't delete any feeds – any feeds that were in it just become uncategorised.

Appearance

Turn Dark mode on or off. This applies to the whole app.

Articles

Choose how long to keep articles you've read, from 7 days to 180 days (30 days to begin with). Read articles older than this are deleted automatically, so the app doesn't keep growing. Articles you haven't read are never deleted. Articles older than this period are also skipped when the app checks your feeds, so they won't appear in your list.

Import Subscriptions

If you're moving from Newsify or another feed reader, you can bring all of your subscriptions across in one go rather than adding them one by one. First, export your subscriptions from the other app as an OPML file (most feed readers offer this) and save it somewhere your phone can reach, such as Files or a cloud storage app.

Then tap Import OPML file, choose the file, check the number of feeds found, and tap Import. Folders from the other app become categories, reusing any category you already have with the same name. Feeds you're already subscribed to are skipped. The new feeds' latest articles are fetched straight away and appear on the Home screen.

Tip: an import can bring in a lot of unread articles at once. If you'd rather start fresh, use Mark all read on the Home screen once they've arrived.

Backup and Restore

RSS News Reader keeps your data only on your phone, so a backup is the only way to get it back if your phone is lost, replaced or reset. The line at the top shows when you last backed up.

Export backup saves your subscriptions, categories, reading history and settings to a file, which you can then save anywhere you like – your phone, a cloud storage app, email, wherever you choose.

Restore backup lets you pick a previously exported file and load it back in. The file is checked first, and nothing changes if it isn't a valid backup. Restoring replaces everything currently in the app, so you'll be asked to confirm first.

Tip: backup files aren't encrypted and contain a record of the feeds you follow and the articles you've read, so keep them somewhere you trust.

Backup Reminders

Turn on Remind me to back up, then use the − and + buttons with weeks, months or years to choose how often (for example, every 2 weeks). The date of the next reminder is shown underneath. When a backup is due, the app shows a reminder the next time you open it: tap Backup to back up there and then, or Skip This Time to be reminded again after the next interval. These reminders appear inside the app only – they aren't phone notifications.

App Reset

Reset all data completely wipes the app – your subscriptions, categories, articles and reading history – and returns every setting to its default, then shows the welcome screen again. This cannot be undone, so the app first offers to make a backup, then asks you to confirm.

🔧Troubleshooting

"No feeds found" when I type a website address

Not every website publishes a news feed. Try the website's home page rather than a specific article, or search for the website by name instead so Feedly can suggest its feed.

"Feeds couldn't be updated" on the Home screen

The app couldn't reach one or more of your feeds the last time it checked. Usually this is because your phone is offline or the website is temporarily down – pull down on the Home screen to try again later. If the same feed keeps failing, open Manage Subscriptions and tap the feed marked ⚠ Couldn't update to see why. If the website has moved its feed, remove the subscription and add the website again.

An article I'd read has disappeared

That's expected: read articles are removed from the Home screen straight away and move to the Read Articles screen, where you can open them again or mark them as unread. They're deleted altogether once they're older than the period set under Articles in Settings.
